How to film for the best results
  • Face-on: stand about 3 to 4 metres in front of the golfer, the camera at hip height and square to the target line.
  • Down-the-line: stand about 3 to 4 metres behind the golfer, on the line from the ball to the target, the camera at hand height.
  • Keep the whole body and the club in frame from head to toes, and hold the camera still on a tripod.
  • Use slow motion (120 or 240 frames a second) and set Video speed to match. A swing is over in about a second and a half, and the downswing lasts roughly a quarter of a second.
  • Start recording a second or two before the swing, stand still at address, and keep recording until the finish.

About the pro benchmark and the 10-point score
  • I could not view professional footage. The benchmark comes from published tempo research and coaching accounts of tour swings.
  • Tempo: tour players are commonly reported to swing at about 3 to 1, backswing to downswing, with a full swing around 1 second and a downswing of roughly 0.25 to 0.3 seconds. Short-game swings run lower, around 1.7 to 2.5 to 1.
  • The ghost swings at 0.75 s back and 0.25 s down from the moment the golfer starts the takeaway.
  • 10 means inside the benchmark range for tempo and consistency, head steadiness, turn, weight shift, balance, spine angle, hip position and knee flex. Those ranges are my coaching estimates, not published measurements of professionals.
  • Pose tracking cannot see the club or ball, so Coach cannot judge clubface, swing path or ball flight.

Live Beach Challenge

Put your camera on a tripod, face-on or down-the-line, so it sees you from head to toes and the club. Press Start challenge, stand still over the ball, and wait for the coach to say Swing when you are ready. Coach reads each swing, times your tempo, gives an instant tip and records the whole session. On a laptop or desktop the built-in or USB webcam works directly. On a phone, open this page over HTTPS in Safari or Chrome.
